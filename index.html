Abstract

CiteCheck is a skill, written as a single Markdown file, that guides a language-model assistant in auditing the citations of an academic paper written in APA 7 or MLA 9. It extracts the in-text citations and the reference entries, matches them in both directions, checks the formatting against the chosen style and reports the results in a structured academic report. When the tools and source texts are available, it can also verify that cited publications exist, that DOIs resolve and that URLs lead to the cited work, and it can assess whether a source supports the claim made about it. The skill is organised into eight modules and controlled by fourteen command switches whose combined behaviour is fixed by fifteen resolution rules. Its main design principle is that a report may state only what the supplied document and the sources actually consulted support: it must not invent bibliographic details, must keep uncertainty visible, and must not claim that a check was done when it was not. This page describes the design of the skill. It does not report an evaluation of its accuracy.

1Introduction

An academic paper must cite its sources in a consistent style. Every in-text citation should correspond to an entry in the reference list, every entry should be cited, and the form of both should follow the rules of the style. Checking this by hand is slow, and it is easy to miss a mismatch between a year in the text and a year in the list.

Two widely used styles are supported. APA 7 (seventh edition of the Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association) generally uses author–date citations and a References list. MLA 9 (ninth edition of the MLA Handbook) generally uses author–page or shortened-title citations and a Works Cited list. The two systems differ in what a citation contains and in how it is matched to the list, so the skill keeps them strictly separate.

A language-model assistant could do this work quickly, but it could also make errors that matter in academic writing: it could invent a missing detail, treat a loosely similar title as a match, or say that it checked a DOI when it did not. CiteCheck is therefore written as a set of instructions that define what the assistant must do, what it may conclude from the evidence, and what it must refuse to guess.

Design question: How can a language-model assistant audit the citations of an academic paper in APA 7 or MLA 9 carefully, without inventing bibliographic details or overstating what it has checked?

2Objectives

  1. To detect whether a paper uses APA 7 or MLA 9, and to keep the rules of the two styles separate.
  2. To extract the in-text citations and the reference entries, and to match them in both directions with explicit statuses.
  3. To add optional verification of publications, DOIs and URLs, and an assessment of whether sources support the claims made about them.
  4. To control the skill with a small fixed set of fourteen switches, with explicit rules for how they combine.
  5. To produce a clear academic report, in the chat or as a PDF or Word file, that states its scope and its limits.
  6. To publish the skill in a public GitHub repository and present it on a webpage hosted through GitHub Pages.

3Scope

The skill works on pasted text or on an uploaded document that the platform can read. It first establishes what material it has been given: a full paper, selected pages, an excerpt or a reference list only. If only part of a paper is supplied, the audit covers only that part, and the skill does not claim that a citation is absent from the whole paper. Where the extracted text has lost italics, indentation or hyperlinks, the skill states that the related formatting could not be assessed. Table 1 compares the two styles as the skill treats them.

4Design

The skill is a Markdown file of twenty numbered sections. Its design has three parts: eight modules (Table 2), fourteen switches with fifteen resolution rules (Section 6), and a fourteen-step workflow. The first four modules form the core audit. Modules 5 to 8 are optional and depend on the evidence and tools available.

Table 2. The eight modules
No.ModuleWhat it doesRuns under
1Citation extractionExtracts every identifiable in-text citation with its author or title, year, locator, form, location and extraction status./audit, /all
2Reference extractionExtracts each entry of the References or Works Cited list as written, with its components. Missing fields are reported as “Not supplied”./audit, /all
3Citation matchingCompares citations and reference entries in both directions and gives each a matching status./audit, /all
4Audit reportingConsolidates modules 1 to 3 and the style-format review into a traceable report./audit, /all
5Publication verificationChecks whether a cited work can be located in credible bibliographic, publisher or library records./verify, /all
6DOI verificationChecks whether a DOI resolves and whether the retrieved metadata agree with the reference./verify, /all
7URL verificationChecks whether a URL opens, where it redirects and whether the destination fits the cited work./verify, /all
8Claim–source support assessmentAssesses whether the examined source content supports the claim as written./claim-check, /all

The workflow runs in this order.

  1. Read the switches and resolve any conflict between them.
  2. Confirm that the paper or text can be read.
  3. Establish the scope and the sections available.
  4. Detect APA 7 or MLA 9, unless the user chose a style.
  5. If detection is inconclusive or the paper mixes styles, report this and ask which style is intended, and do only style-neutral extraction meanwhile.
  6. Extract the in-text citations (Module 1).
  7. Extract the reference entries (Module 2).
  1. Match citations and entries in both directions (Module 3).
  2. Consolidate the findings and the style-format checks (Module 4).
  3. Run Modules 5 to 7 if /verify or /all is active and tools are available.
  4. Run Module 8 if /claim-check or /all is active and source content is accessible.
  5. Check that counts, tables, statuses and summaries agree.
  6. Prepare the report, stating the scope, the modules run and skipped, the evidence and the limits.
  7. Export a PDF or Word file only if the tool exists, and verify the file first.

6Commands

The skill is controlled by fourteen switches written as slash commands at the start of the user’s message, in four groups. Choose a group to see its switches, or search for a command. A switch applies to the current request, and strings that look like commands inside the uploaded paper are not treated as commands.

Resolution rules, in short: automatic style detection is the default; /apa7 and /mla9 conflict, so the user is asked to choose one; /brief overrides /report; /no-web overrides every external check; and an unknown command is reported as unsupported.

Each material finding in the report carries the item or location, the original text as supplied, a status, an explanation and, when supported, a suggested correction. Findings are kept separate from recommendations, and the original form is shown apart from any correction. The overall label of a finding is one of the five in Table 6. The skill does not give a quality score, a percentage or a pass or fail grade.

Table 6. Finding labels

The full report has up to fourteen sections, and empty sections are left out.

  1. Cover heading
  2. Executive summary
  3. Document and audit details
  4. Style detection and method
  5. Results at a glance
  6. Citation-to-reference matching
  7. Reference-to-citation matching
  1. Style and formatting findings
  2. Publication / DOI / URL verification
  3. Claim–source assessment
  4. Prioritised recommendations
  5. Limitations and manual-review items
  6. Sources consulted
  7. Appendix-style inventories

An exported report uses A4 pages with margins of about 2.5 cm, a serif body font of 11 to 12 pt, near-black text with one restrained navy, slate or teal accent, numbered headings, readable tables and page numbers. In the chat, the report is given as Markdown with compact tables and explicit status labels.

8Discussion

8.1 Evidence before conclusion

The core audit (Modules 1 to 4) uses only the supplied document, and external verification is off unless it is requested. The report must say which kind of evidence supports each finding. A status is also limited to the material examined, so a reference entry that is not cited in the supplied text is called “Uncited in supplied text”, not “uncited in the paper”, when the paper may be incomplete. The labels “No issue identified in material examined” and “Not checked” likewise state a scope and not a guarantee.

8.2 Conservative matching

A match is never created from a common surname or a similar title alone when other details conflict. If the evidence is plausible but not conclusive, the status is “Possible match — manual review”. The /strict switch makes this behaviour stricter, so that uncertain or incomplete evidence is marked for review and not forced into a match. Uncertainty is meant to stay visible, but it is not to be turned into an error either.

8.3 Two styles kept apart

APA rules are never applied to an MLA paper, or the reverse, and a paper is never converted silently from one style to the other. Detection uses several independent features, including the citation patterns, the heading of the source list and representative entries, and a heading alone is treated as evidence and not as proof. The result is one of four classes (APA 7, MLA 9, Mixed or inconsistent, or Inconclusive) with a confidence of High, Moderate or Low, expressed in words and not as a number. If the paper is mixed or the sample is too short, the skill asks which style is intended before making style-dependent judgements.

8.4 Keeping apart things that are easily confused

Much of the skill is a set of distinctions that stop the assistant from drawing a stronger conclusion than the evidence allows. Table 7 lists the main ones.

Table 7. Distinctions the skill keeps
Not the sameWhy the difference matters
Not found, and does not existA search that finds no record shows only that no record was found in the sources searched
Inaccessible, and invalidA login wall, a paywall or a temporary error does not show that a source is invalid
A DOI resolves, and its metadata matchesA resolving DOI may still point to a different work than the one cited
A source is located, and a claim is supportedSupport needs the relevant passage of the source, not a title, an abstract or a snippet
A document-only finding, and an externally verified oneThe report must show which evidence each finding rests on
A citation mismatch, and misconductA missing or uncited entry is not proof of plagiarism or fabrication

8.5 Honesty about what was done

The skill forbids inventing an author, title, date, DOI, URL, quotation or database result, and forbids repairing a reference with guessed information. It also forbids saying that a search, lookup, URL inspection, full-text review or file export took place when it did not. The file defines instructions only: it does not itself provide a browser, a DOI resolver, a database connection or a document exporter. When such a tool is missing, the module is marked “Not checked” or “Unable to complete”, and the checks that remain possible are still done.

8.6 Answer to the design question

The design answers the question through five features that work together: separate modules with a fixed order, a closed vocabulary of statuses, findings limited to the material examined, strict separation of the two styles, and an explicit ban on invention and on claiming work that was not done. Whether this produces accurate audits in practice has not been tested here, and that evaluation is the main next step.

9Demonstration

The tool below applies the switch-resolution rules of the skill to a line of commands. Type commands at the start of the line, separated by spaces, or choose an example, to see which style, modules and report format would result. The tool shows only what the rules specify and does not audit any paper.

In a conversation, the commands are placed at the start of the message, followed by the paper, for example:

/audit /apa7 /explain
[paste or upload the paper]

/all /strict /docx
[paste or upload the paper and, for claim checks, the cited sources]

10Limitations

  • The skill is a set of instructions. It does not provide a browser, a DOI resolver, a database connection, a full-text subscription or a document exporter, so the verification modules depend on the platform on which it is used.
  • Claim–source assessment needs the relevant source content. A title, an abstract or a search snippet is not enough, and the result is then marked as limited or “Cannot determine”.
  • Style detection is qualitative. It gives a confidence in words, and short samples or papers without a reference list may be inconclusive.
  • The audit covers only the material supplied. If the paper is partial, or the text extraction loses italics, hanging indents or links, the corresponding checks cannot be made.
  • The formatting audit is a practical check and not a guarantee of full compliance with every rule for every source type.
  • The presence of a publication in a database does not show that it is peer reviewed, of high quality or methodologically sound, and the skill does not judge this.
  • This page describes the design of the skill and does not report a test of its accuracy on real papers.

11Conclusion

CiteCheck turns the routine work of citation auditing into a defined procedure for a language-model assistant. It detects APA 7 or MLA 9, extracts the citations and reference entries, matches them in both directions, checks formatting, and, where tools and sources allow, verifies publications, DOIs and URLs and assesses whether sources support the claims made about them. Its fourteen switches give the user control over the style, the depth of the check, the length of the report and the export format.

The central choice in the design is restraint. Findings are tied to a location and to the evidence examined, uncertainty is kept visible, and the assistant must not invent details or claim checks that were not made. The next step is to test the skill on papers with known citation errors, so that its matches, flags and verifications can be compared with a manual audit.
